### The core idea

Think of it as:

> **Sanjit Majumdar — Engineer + Builder**
>
> A portfolio that proves what I can do instead of just telling people what I can do.

The homepage should immediately communicate:

* **Who you are**
* **What you build**
* **How you think**
* **What you've accomplished**
* **What you're technically capable of**
* **What you're currently building**
* **Actual things people can interact with**

And then the rest of the site becomes your **public engineering playground**.

But there's an important distinction:

### **Work ≠ Projects ≠ Lab**

That's what would make this much stronger than a typical portfolio.

**Work**

> Things I've built professionally.

Show real engineering depth, architecture, scale, problems solved, decisions made, and measurable impact.

**Projects**

> Things I'm building because I believe they should exist.

CityFix, Surakkha, personal finance systems, etc.

**Lab**

> Things I built because I wanted to experiment.

Calculators, games, AI experiments, tiny utilities, visualizations, weird ideas.

This lets a recruiter understand your professional experience without having to dig through 40 random projects.

# 5. Engineering philosophy

This could differentiate you considerably.

Something like:

> **I care about why something is built, not just how.**

Then 3–4 principles:

**Simple beats clever**

> If the system can be understood by the next engineer, that's usually the better architecture.

**Performance is a feature**

> Don't optimize everything. Find the expensive things and fix those.

**AI is a tool, not an architect**

> I use AI aggressively for implementation, exploration and review — but architecture and engineering decisions still need reasoning.

**Build for the real world**

> Networks fail. Requirements change. Users do unexpected things. Production doesn't care about your happy path.

This is the kind of section that makes the portfolio feel like **an engineer's portfolio rather than a résumé rendered in HTML**.

# The bigger idea

I'd actually give the whole thing a name.

Not necessarily `sanjit.dev`.

Something like:

### **Sanjit Labs**

```text
SANJIT
ENGINEERING

Build.
Experiment.
Solve.

[ Work ] [ Lab ] [ Projects ] [ About ]
```

Then your site becomes a platform:

```text
                 SANJIT
                   │
        ┌──────────┼──────────┐
        │          │          │
      WORK      PROJECTS      LAB
        │          │          │
   Enterprise   CityFix     Tools
   Wellbook     Surakkha    Games
   APIs         Products    Experiments
   Architecture AI          Weird stuff
```

And that's the part I think is particularly valuable for your career goal.

You're not trying to make a website that **claims you're a Tech Lead**.

You're building a website that gives someone enough evidence to **reach that conclusion themselves**.

If we build this properly, the project itself can demonstrate **architecture, UI engineering, backend engineering, product thinking, AI usage, performance, testing, CI/CD, documentation and leadership** — all in one portfolio.
