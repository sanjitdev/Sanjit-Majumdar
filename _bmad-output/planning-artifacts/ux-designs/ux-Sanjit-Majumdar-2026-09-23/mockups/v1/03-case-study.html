Case study · 01 · Enterprise governance

Wellbook — Enterprise governance for an oil & gas organization.

Three ERPs, one truth that didn't exist. A canonical schema, a 14-hour migration window, and 10K+ daily users who never saw a regression.

01 · Problem

Three ERPs, one truth that doesn't exist.

Procurement, finance, and a vendor portal — each owned its own copy of "vendor." No two agreed on schema. Every integration was a translation table. Every translation table drifted.

Worse: business-critical workflows stalled on reconciliation. A vendor update in procurement took three days to surface in finance. The Wellbook team had decided to stop losing those days.

02 · Constraints

No replace. Migration window of 14 hours.

Production cutover happened during the 03:50–08:30 EDT low-traffic window. The platform had to remain queryable the entire time. No rollback plan was acceptable — only forward progress with checkpoints.

The two ERPs we couldn't touch kept their schemas intact. The translation had to happen at our layer, not theirs.

03 · Architecture

One canonical schema. Map at the boundary.

We defined a single normalized representation that all three ERPs agreed on. We translated at integration boundaries, never inside the domain. We validated before write — failed closed, with the reason surfaced.

The schema was versioned. Changes shipped with a migration, never silently. Every rejected write became an alert we could route to a human, not a poison-pill queue nobody sees.

04 · Decisions

The decisions that mattered.

Decision · Audit replay

Every event sourced into the canonical model is reproducible from the immutable log. We can replay any 90-day window deterministically. This is what made the migration safe.

Decision · Syncfusion Data-Grid instead of a third-party dashboard

Removed a $74k/year licensing dependency. Cut bundle size 35%. Got the exact rendering the operations team needed.

Decision · Ship-faster pushback on the December deadline

QA wanted a December ship date. The system wasn't ready. We documented the call, deferred with receipts, and shipped in February with zero P1 incidents. See: Ship-Faster Pushback.

05 · Challenges

What nearly killed it.

Two weeks before cutover, finance flagged a timezone-handling bug in the canonical adapter. We had assumed UTC; one ERP was sending local time without offsets. The fix took three days and a comprehensive integration test pass.

The post-mortem became a checklist we now apply to every boundary: timezones, currency precision, character encoding, idempotency keys. It sits next to the deployment runbook.
