01 · Problem

Three ERPs, three sources of truth, one organisation.

The client — an international oil & gas organisation — had grown through acquisition. Three different ERPs, three different project-management vocabularies, three finance teams that refused to reconcile at month-end. Reporting took three weeks of manual spreadsheet stitching.

Wellbook had to be a single source of truth for project governance — from field operations through finance — without forcing the legacy systems to be replaced. The legacy ERPs stayed; Wellbook sat on top.

02 · Approach

Canonical schema first. Then everything else.

The first three months were schema work. We modelled procurement, projects, vendors, finance events, and document registers as canonical entities with stable IDs. The three ERPs became adapters — each one mapped into the canonical model with explicit translation rules.

Field operations used a single Angular SPA against the canonical API. Legacy systems kept their own UIs for deep workflows they did well; Wellbook became the place where governance happened — authorisations, audit trails, regulator reporting.

The adapters are the load-bearing piece. They run as scheduled jobs and as event listeners; each one owns its translation rules and reconciliation windows. When ERP A disagrees with canonical, ERP A wins on its own fields but canonical wins on the governed fields. That's the rule that kept everyone sane.

04 · Decisions and tradeoffs

The calls I made — and the ones I'd reconsider.

Decision

Canonical schema as the contract

Three months of schema before any UI. Tedious, but every later decision got easier.

Why

Without a stable vocabulary, every adapter becomes a one-off. The schema paid for itself in the first integration.

Tradeoff

Three months of "no visible progress" to non-technical stakeholders. Required real political capital.

Decision

Adapters, not replacements

ERPs stayed. Wellbook adapted into them, didn't replace them.

Why

Replacing any of the three ERPs would have been a 3-year program with no delivery in between.

Tradeoff

Three sets of credentials to manage, three audit logs to reconcile. Real operational overhead.

Decision

SQL Server, not Postgres

Stayed on the client's existing SQL Server expertise and licensing.

Why

DBAs were already on staff. Migration cost outweighed any technical advantage of switching.

Tradeoff

Multi-tenant patterns are more constrained than on Postgres. Acceptable; documented.
